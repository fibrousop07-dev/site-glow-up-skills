# AI cadence patterns and plain rewrites

Use as a spotting list, not a ban list. A pattern is fine once, when it says something true.

## Words to question
elevate, unlock, supercharge, empower, leverage, seamless, seamlessly, robust, cutting-edge, game-changing, revolutionary, next-level, streamline, harness, unleash, transform, navigate (figurative), landscape, journey, tapestry, delve, holistic, synergy, "world-class", "best-in-class", "state-of-the-art", "all-in-one", "end-to-end", "at scale", "effortless".
Fix: say the concrete thing. "Seamless integration" becomes "Works with Shopify and Stripe."

## Structures
| Pattern | Example | Plain rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Not just X, but Y | "Not just a website, but a growth engine" | "A website that brings in customers." |
| Rule-of-three by reflex | "Fast, reliable, and secure" | Keep the one that's true and prove it: "Pages load in under a second." |
| Fast-paced-world opener | "In today's fast-paced digital world..." | Delete. Start with the point. |
| Rhetorical question lead | "Ready to take your business to the next level?" | A direct offer: "Get a quote within a day." |
| Em-dash aside | "We build apps — fast, beautiful, yours — for startups" | Two short sentences. |
| Vague superlative | "The ultimate solution" | A measurable claim or nothing. |
| Abstract noun stack | "Innovative solutions for modern challenges" | What, for whom: "Booking software for dental clinics." |
| Hollow promise | "Experience the difference" | What the difference is. |
| Mirrored clauses | "We don't just X. We Y." | Say Y. |
| Everything is a journey/ecosystem/platform | "Your all-in-one ecosystem" | Name the product type. |

## CTAs
Weak: Get started, Learn more, Discover more, Explore, Submit, Click here.
Specific: Book a 20-minute call, See pricing, Download the spec sheet, Send the brief, Try the demo, Call 555-0100.
Match the CTA to the next real step, and the button label to what the page after the click does.

## Headlines
- Name the thing and its benefit in the reader's words. Ask: could a competitor put this exact headline on their site? If yes, it isn't done.
- Prefer a noun and a verb over adjectives. 6-10 words is usually enough.
- One idea per headline. Cut the pill/badge text above it unless it's real news.

## Paragraphs
- First sentence carries the point. 1-3 sentences for a feature blurb, 3-5 for about text.
- Use numbers, names, places, materials, and times from the actual business. If the facts aren't on the page, ask the user for them rather than inventing.
- Vary sentence length. Fragments are allowed. Read it aloud: if you'd never say it to a customer, change it.

## Voice matching
Take 2-3 lines of the most human existing copy (or the user's own writing) as the model. Note: person (we/I), formality, sentence length, humor. Apply consistently; don't flatten to generic "friendly professional".

## Humanizer tuned for cold emails: things to undo
It may add openers ("Quick note:", "One thing I noticed:"), shorten too aggressively, or make text conversational in a way that doesn't fit a legal page or formal brand. Compare and keep the removals of buzzwords and em dashes; drop the email-isms.
